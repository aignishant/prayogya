## One-line answer

A budget is a ceiling on model calls for one unit of work — one bin count — counted across every
agent that touches it and enforced by raising, never by clamping, because a ceiling that quietly
returns zero is a ceiling that lets the fifth call happen and records that it did not.

## The idea

The radio kit has a battery, and the battery is the only part of the kit that runs out. A counter
who keeps pressing the talk button after the battery is flat is not making calls that fail loudly;
they are making calls that go nowhere, while the count sheet fills up with quantities the ledger
never heard. The supervisor's rule is simple: when the radio says *low battery*, you stop and come
back to the desk. You do not keep talking. You do not write down what the ledger *would have said*.

This desk will spend model calls the way a counter spends battery, and by day 10 there will be
four agents doing the spending on one bin — a supervisor that routes, a counter that reads the
sheet, a reconciler that explains the gap, a replenisher that raises the order. Four agents is at
least four calls per bin. Two agents that talk to each other — a counter and a reconciler
disagreeing about a variance — is an unbounded number of calls until something bounds it. That
something is a **budget**, and three choices about it are made now, before any agent exists,
because they are the choices that cannot be retrofitted.

**It counts calls.** Not tokens, not money. A token count is a fact about one provider's tokeniser
and money is a fact about one provider's price list; a call is the one unit every provider, every
stand-in and every person agrees on, and it is the one a reader can hold in their head while
reading a trace: *this bin cost four calls.*

**It is per unit of work.** One budget per bin count, not one per night. A budget shared across
the night's six hundred counts would let one bin that loops eat the allowance for the other five
hundred and ninety-nine, and the failure would arrive as *the ledger stopped answering at bin
four hundred* rather than *bin seventeen looped*. The label on the budget says which unit, and it
is in every refusal.

**It refuses by raising.** When the ceiling is reached, `spend` raises `BudgetExceeded` — with who
asked, which call number, what the ceiling was, and who had already spent what. It does not return
zero and let the caller decide. A caller that gets a zero and carries on is the counter still
pressing the button, and the failure section shows exactly what that looks like: a budget that
reports four calls spent when six were made.

## The mechanism

The whole file, at `stock_desk/util/budget.py`:

```python
"""The ceiling on model calls, counted across the whole cast and enforced by refusing.

This desk ends up with four agents working one count. Four agents is at least four model calls,
and a counter and a reconciler that talk to each other is unbounded until something bounds it.
That something is this file.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass, field


class BudgetExceeded(RuntimeError):
    """The work asked for more model calls than it was allowed.

    Raised rather than returned, and never swallowed into a default answer: the caller is meant
    to stop, not to continue with a count it could not finish.
    """


@dataclass
class Budget:
    """A ceiling for one unit of work, and a record of who spent what.

    `limit` is calls, not tokens and not money — a count is the only unit every provider agrees
    on, and it is the one a person can hold in their head while reading a trace.
    """

    limit: int
    label: str = "one count"
    spent: int = 0
    by: dict[str, int] = field(default_factory=dict)

    def spend(self, who: str, calls: int = 1) -> int:
        """Record `calls` against `who`, or refuse. Returns what is left."""
        if self.spent + calls > self.limit:
            raise BudgetExceeded(
                f"{who} asked for call {self.spent + calls} of {self.limit} for {self.label}; "
                f"already spent: {self.by}"
            )
        self.spent += calls
        self.by[who] = self.by.get(who, 0) + calls
        return self.remaining

    @property
    def remaining(self) -> int:
        return self.limit - self.spent
```

**Line by line:**

- `class BudgetExceeded(RuntimeError)` — **a named refusal**, distinct from every other error, so a
  caller can catch this one and stop. The docstring is an instruction to that caller: stop, do not
  substitute.
- `@dataclass class Budget` — **a record with one method.** A dataclass because a budget is mostly
  data — a limit, a label, a count, a breakdown — and the generated `__init__` and `__repr__` are
  exactly what a log line or a test wants to see.
- `limit: int` — **calls.** The docstring says why it is not tokens, and the type says it is a
  whole number: there is no such thing as half a call.
- `label: str = "one count"` — **the unit of work, named**, and it appears in every refusal. A
  refusal that says *budget exceeded* sends a reader to the log; one that says *for count of bin
  C-07-2* sends them to the bin.
- `by: dict[str, int] = field(default_factory=dict)` — **who spent what**, and `default_factory`
  rather than `= {}` because a mutable default on a dataclass field is one dictionary shared by
  every budget ever made — a ruff rule refuses it, and so does anyone who has debugged it.
- `if self.spent + calls > self.limit: raise BudgetExceeded(...)` — **check, then refuse, before
  recording.** The count is not incremented on a refusal, so a refused budget still reports the
  truth: four spent, not five.
- `f"{who} asked for call {self.spent + calls} of {self.limit} for {self.label}; already spent:
  {self.by}"` — **everything a reader needs, in the exception.** Which agent, which call number,
  the ceiling, the unit, and the breakdown of the calls that got there. A trace with this line in
  it needs no other line to explain why the count stopped.
- `self.by[who] = self.by.get(who, 0) + calls` — **the breakdown, by name.** On day 10 this is
  what says the reconciler made three of the four calls, which is the fact that tells somebody
  where the loop is.
- `return self.remaining` — **what is left, so a caller can pass it on.** The workflow runtime on
  day 11 hands the framework a hard bound, and the number it hands is this.

Run it. Four agents, one call each, on one bin — and a fifth call from an agent that has already
had its turn:

```bash
uv run python -c "
from stock_desk.util.budget import Budget, BudgetExceeded
b = Budget(limit=4, label='count of bin C-07-2')
for who in ('supervisor', 'counter', 'reconciler', 'replenisher'):
    print(who, 'left', b.spend(who))
try:
    b.spend('reconciler')
except BudgetExceeded as refused:
    print('refused:', refused)
"
```

```
supervisor left 3
counter left 2
reconciler left 1
replenisher left 0
refused: reconciler asked for call 5 of 4 for count of bin C-07-2; already spent: {'supervisor': 1, 'counter': 1, 'reconciler': 1, 'replenisher': 1}
```

**Line by line:**

- `supervisor left 3` … `replenisher left 0` — **the return value is the remaining count**, and it
  reaches zero on the fourth call with nothing refused. Four agents, one call each, is the shape
  of a healthy count on this desk.
- `refused: reconciler asked for call 5 of 4` — **the fifth call is refused, and the message says
  who asked.** The reconciler had already spent its one; it came back for another, which is what a
  reconciler does when it disagrees with the counter, and the budget is the thing that says *not
  on this bin*.
- `already spent: {'supervisor': 1, 'counter': 1, 'reconciler': 1, 'replenisher': 1}` — **the
  breakdown, in the refusal.** Nobody has to go and find the trace; the shape of the count is in
  the sentence.

## When it breaks

The friendly version. A budget that clamps — returns zero when the ceiling is reached and lets
the caller carry on — because raising "seems harsh":

```diff

And what that version records when the reconciler comes back twice more:

```
supervisor left 3
counter left 2
reconciler left 1

Six calls were made. The budget says four. The reconciler's two extra calls went to the provider —
nothing in this version stopped them; it only stopped *counting* them — and the record that a
person reads afterwards says the bin cost four and the reconciler made one. That is the counter
pressing the flat radio's button: the calls happen, the sheet does not show them, and the bill at
the end of the month is the first thing that does. The test is red because it asked for a raise
and got a zero; the desk would be wrong because it asked for a ceiling and got a counter that
stops at the ceiling and lets the work go on. The smallest fix is the `raise`, and the principle
is in the exception's docstring: **the caller is meant to stop.**

## In production

**What a senior writes instead.** The same dataclass, with the budget attached to the *request*
rather than passed by hand — a context variable that every agent reads, so that a call made three
workflows deep is charged without the code between knowing a budget exists. Day 12 of this project
does exactly that, and the reason it is not done today is that the plumbing to do it does not exist
yet and a budget passed by hand is the version whose refusal is easiest to see.

**What degrades at scale.** A budget of four per bin is sixty thousand bins times four, and the
night the reconciler starts looping is the night the provider's quota is spent by bin eleven
thousand — with a per-bin ceiling, every one of those bins stops at four and the queue finishes;
without one, or with a per-night budget, the loop eats the allowance and forty-nine thousand bins
are never counted at all, and the morning's first question is *which bin* and there is no answer.
A per-unit ceiling is what makes a runaway a local fact about one bin rather than a global fact
about the night.

**The review comment.** *"This diff raises `limit` from 4 to 12 for the reconciler 'because it
needs more room'. A budget is per bin, so that is twelve calls on every bin, not on the ones that
need it. If the reconciler legitimately needs three calls on a hard bin, the ceiling is 6 and the
reconciler's loop is the thing to look at."*

**The interview question.** *"Four agents cooperate on one task and two of them can call each
other. How do you bound what one task costs, where does the bound live, and what happens — exactly
— on the call that would exceed it?"*
