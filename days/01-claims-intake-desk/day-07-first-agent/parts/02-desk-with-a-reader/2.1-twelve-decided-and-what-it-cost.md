## One-line answer

Four fast-tracked, eight deficiency letters with eight different reasons, nothing pending — the
sentence `PROJECT.md` committed to on day 0 — and the model is asked last, so a notification the
fields already settle costs nothing.

## The idea

The interpreter is expensive and is not always needed. A caller who has not given a date of loss
does not need translating: the form has a blank on it, and the blank is the answer whatever the
caller said about the water.

So the handler works in an order. Everything on the form first — is there a date, a contact, a
policy number that exists, was it on risk — and only then, if the claim is still live, does the
interpreter get put on the line.

That ordering is worth money, and it is worth being explicit that money is the reason. The rules are
free. The model is not: it is a request, a bill, a rate limit and a wait, and it is the only thing
in this desk that has any of those properties. **Asking it last means asking it only when the answer
depends on it.**

Today the desk does that, and two things fall out. The pending tray empties — every notification now
reaches a verdict — and the queue produces exactly the numbers day 0 promised, which is the first
time in this project that a sentence written before any code existed has been checked against a run.

There is also a quieter change, and it is the boundary's doing. Day 2's rules took a `Policy` — the
whole stored record. The desk does not have policies any more; it has whatever the boundary chose
to hand over. So the rules now take a **`Cover`**: in force, perils, limit. Three fields, because
three fields is what the rules ever looked at, and the boundary was right not to send more.

## The mechanism

First the domain gains the shape the boundary hands over. A **marked diff against
`claims_desk/domain.py` as printed on day 2 part 2.1**:

**Line by line:**

- **`Cover` is three fields, and that is the whole point.** It is not a smaller `Policy`; it is the
  answer to the only question the rules ever asked one — was this on risk, for this peril, up to how
  much. `holder_ref`, `risk_address`, `product` and `excess` are absent because no rule reads them.
- **The boundary decided this shape, not the domain.** Day 3 chose what `fetch_policy` returns; the
  desk receives that and nothing else; and the rules had to be reshaped to fit what crosses the
  line. That is a boundary doing what a boundary is for, arriving as a small refactor four days
  later.
- `from_policy(cls, policy, day)` — cover computed from a stored record, **taking the day**, because
  `in_force` is a fact about a date rather than about a policy. Only the tests that read the store
  directly use it.
- `from_boundary(cls, answer, in_force)` — **two arguments, because the boundary answers two
  questions.** `fetch_policy` says what is covered; `check_in_force` says whether it was on risk.
  Neither tool answers both, which is a design from day 5 showing up here as a two-argument
  constructor.
- `answer.get("perils", ())` and `int(answer.get("fast_track_limit", 0))` — **defensive on the way
  in**, because this is data that crossed a process line. A boundary that changed its answer shape
  produces an empty cover rather than a `KeyError` in the middle of a claim.
- `if not cover.in_force:` replacing `if not policy.in_force_on(...)` — the *computation* moved to
  the boundary and the *rule* stayed here. That is the right split: the boundary owns the policy
  data, the desk owns what the answer means.
- **Everything else in `assess` is unchanged**, including the order. The rules did not change today;
  only what they are given did.

Then the desk. The whole file, at `claims_desk/desk.py` — day 6 printed a version of this with
`rules_that_need_no_reading` in it, and that function is gone:

```python
"""The desk itself: read the queue, ask the boundary, ask the classifier, decide.

Day 6 could settle four of twelve notifications and had to return `pending` for the rest, because
four of day 2's eight rules need somebody to have read the sentence a caller spoke. Day 7 supplies
that somebody, and the shape of this file changes in two ways worth noticing.

`rules_that_need_no_reading` is gone. It duplicated the *order* of day 2's rules and admitted so
in its own docstring; now that the desk has every fact, it calls `assess` and there is one place
where the order lives.

And the model call is made **last**. Every rule that can be decided from fields is decided first,
so a notification with no date of loss costs nothing. A model call is the most expensive thing this
desk does and the only one it pays for.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass

from claims_desk import boundary
from claims_desk.agents import classifier
from claims_desk.domain import Cover, Notification, Outcome, assess
from claims_desk.store import Store
from claims_desk.util.budget import Budget, BudgetExceeded
from claims_desk.util.logging import log

#: The verdict for a notification this desk cannot decide with the facts it has.
PENDING = "pending"

#: What a pending notification is waiting for. Not an error code: a description of the missing
#: input, so that whatever supplies it later knows what it is being asked for.
NEEDS_CLASSIFICATION = "needs_classification"

#: Model calls allowed per notification. One classifier call, and headroom for the retry day 15
#: adds. The ceiling is per notification because that is the unit an insurer costs work in.
CALLS_PER_NOTIFICATION = 2


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Verdict:
    """What the desk concluded about one notification, and why.

    `outcome` is a member of `Outcome`, or `PENDING`. A pending verdict carries `waiting_for`
    instead of `reason`: the first says what the desk needs, the second says what the
    policyholder needs to send.
    """

    claim_id: str
    outcome: str
    reason: str | None = None
    waiting_for: str | None = None

    @property
    def decided(self) -> bool:
        return self.outcome != PENDING


def settled_without_reading(
    notification: Notification, cover: Cover | None
) -> tuple[str, str] | None:
    """Would `assess` reach a verdict without a loss type? If so, return it.

    The four early rules — a missing date, a missing contact, an unknown policy, a policy not in
    force — do not look at the peril or the injury. Asking `assess` with a peril no policy covers
    finds out whether one of them fires, **without a model call and without inventing a fact**:
    the placeholder can only ever produce `peril_not_covered`, which is the answer this function
    treats as "not settled".
    """
    outcome, reason = assess(notification, cover, loss_type="", injury_reported=False)
    if reason is not None and reason.value != "peril_not_covered":
        return outcome.value, reason.value
    return None


async def decide(notification: Notification, budget: Budget) -> Verdict:
    """Reach a verdict on one notification. At most two boundary calls and one model call.

    The order is deliberate and it is about money: everything decidable from fields is decided
    first, and the classifier is asked only when the answer actually depends on it.
    """
    policy = await boundary.fetch_policy(notification.policy_number)
    in_force = False
    if policy.get("found") and notification.date_of_loss is not None:
        answer = await boundary.check_in_force(
            notification.policy_number, notification.date_of_loss
        )
        in_force = bool(answer.get("in_force"))
    cover = Cover.from_boundary(policy, in_force) if policy.get("found") else None

    early = settled_without_reading(notification, cover)
    if early is not None:
        outcome, reason = early
        verdict = Verdict(notification.id, outcome, reason=reason)
        log("desk.decided", claim=verdict.claim_id, outcome=outcome, reason=reason, model=False)
        return verdict

    try:
        read = await classifier.classify(notification.description, budget)
    except BudgetExceeded as refused:
        log("desk.over_budget", claim=notification.id, detail=str(refused))
        return Verdict(notification.id, PENDING, waiting_for="over_budget")

    if "error" in read:
        log("desk.classification_failed", claim=notification.id, detail=read["error"])
        return Verdict(notification.id, PENDING, waiting_for=NEEDS_CLASSIFICATION)

    outcome, reason = assess(notification, cover, read["loss_type"], bool(read["injury_reported"]))
    verdict = Verdict(
        notification.id, outcome.value, reason=reason.value if reason else None
    )
    log(
        "desk.decided",
        claim=verdict.claim_id,
        outcome=verdict.outcome,
        reason=verdict.reason,
        model=True,
        loss_type=read["loss_type"],
    )
    return verdict


async def run_queue(store: Store | None = None) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Decide every notification in the queue and record the ones that were decided.

    Returns a count per outcome. A pending verdict is **not** written to the claim file: the desk
    has not decided anything about that claim, and a claim file saying `pending` would be a
    decision about a claim nobody has looked at.

    Each notification gets its own `Budget`, because the ceiling is per notification. A budget
    shared across the queue would let one looping claim exhaust the whole morning's allowance.
    """
    queue = (store or Store()).notifications()
    counts = {Outcome.FAST_TRACK.value: 0, Outcome.DEFICIENCY.value: 0, PENDING: 0}
    for notification in queue:
        budget = Budget(limit=CALLS_PER_NOTIFICATION, label=f"notification {notification.id}")
        verdict = await decide(notification, budget)
        counts[verdict.outcome] += 1
        if verdict.decided:
            written = await boundary.record_decision(
                verdict.claim_id, verdict.outcome, verdict.reason
            )
            # The boundary refuses a shape it does not recognise, and it refuses as *data*. A
            # caller that does not read the answer counts a decision it never recorded — which is
            # the failure day 6 part 3.2 exists to show. `recorded: false` here is not a claim
            # outcome, it is this desk sending something the boundary does not accept.
            if not written.get("recorded"):
                raise RuntimeError(
                    f"boundary refused to record {verdict.claim_id}: {written.get('refused')}"
                )
    log("desk.queue_finished", **counts)
    return counts
```

**Line by line:**

- **The docstring names both changes and why**, including the deletion. A file that quietly loses a
  function leaves a reader wondering; one that says *"`rules_that_need_no_reading` is gone"* and
  gives the reason does not.
- `CALLS_PER_NOTIFICATION = 2` — **one for the classifier and one spare.** Its comment says the
  spare is for day 15's retry, and it says why the ceiling is per notification rather than per run:
  that is the unit an insurer costs work in, and it is also the unit that contains a runaway.
- `def settled_without_reading(notification, cover) -> tuple[str, str] | None:` — the replacement for
  day 6's duplicated rule list, and it works by **asking the real rules** rather than restating
  them. That is the whole improvement: there is now one place the order lives.
- `assess(notification, cover, loss_type="", injury_reported=False)` — **a placeholder that cannot
  be mistaken for an answer.** The empty string is in no policy's peril list, so the only verdict it
  can produce is `peril_not_covered`.
- `if reason is not None and reason.value != "peril_not_covered": return ...` — **the one result
  this function must discard.** Everything else `assess` can return with an empty loss type is a
  rule that did not look at the loss type, which is precisely the set of rules that are free.
- **Why this is a slightly uncomfortable design, said out loud**: it depends on knowing that
  `peril_not_covered` is the only verdict a placeholder can produce, which is a fact about day 2's
  rule order rather than about this function. It is one indirection better than day 6's version,
  which duplicated the whole order, and it is not the last word.
- `policy = await boundary.fetch_policy(...)` then a conditional `check_in_force` — **unchanged from
  day 6.** The second call is still avoided when there is no policy or no date, and for the same
  reason.
- `cover = Cover.from_boundary(policy, in_force) if policy.get("found") else None` — the two tool
  answers become one `Cover`, or `None` when there is no policy. The `None` is what `assess` turns
  into `UNKNOWN_POLICY`.
- `early = settled_without_reading(...)` **before any model call** — the ordering the whole part is
  about. A notification with no date of loss returns here, and the budget is untouched.
- `log(..., model=False)` on that path and `model=True` on the other — **every decision says whether
  it cost a model call.** One field, and it makes "how many of this morning's claims needed the
  classifier" a question the log can answer.
- `except BudgetExceeded as refused:` — day 1's budget, raising, caught here and turned into a
  **pending verdict with `waiting_for="over_budget"`**. Not a deficiency: the policyholder has done
  nothing wrong, and the desk ran out of allowance. A claim refused for the desk's reasons must not
  be recorded as a claim refused for the claimant's.
- `if "error" in read:` — a classification that failed leaves the claim **pending**, which is day 6's
  machinery still doing its job. The desk did not decide, and it says what it is waiting for.
- `assess(notification, cover, read["loss_type"], bool(read["injury_reported"]))` — the full rules,
  with the model's two facts. `bool(...)` because the value came out of JSON and a model that
  answered `"true"` as a string would otherwise be truthy in a way nobody intended.
- `log(..., loss_type=read["loss_type"])` — **the classification is recorded on the decision line.**
  When a claim is questioned later, the log says which peril the desk thought it was, and that is
  the field a disagreement will be about.
- `budget = Budget(limit=CALLS_PER_NOTIFICATION, label=f"notification {notification.id}")` **inside
  the loop** — one budget per notification, freshly made. The docstring says what a shared one would
  cost: one looping claim exhausting the whole morning.
- **`run_queue` is otherwise unchanged from day 6**, including the refusal check that day's failure
  produced.

## When it breaks

The ordering that saves money can be verified, and so can its absence. Two notifications, one that
needs the classifier and one that does not:

```bash
uv run python -c "
import asyncio
from claims_desk import desk
from claims_desk.store import Store
from claims_desk.util.budget import Budget
q = {n.id: n for n in Store().notifications()}
for cid in ('FNOL-4471','FNOL-4473','FNOL-4478','FNOL-4479','FNOL-4481'):
    b = Budget(limit=2, label=cid)
    v = asyncio.run(desk.decide(q[cid], b))
    print(f'{cid}  {v.outcome:<11} {v.reason or v.waiting_for or \"-\"}   calls={b.spent}')
"
```

```
FNOL-4471  fast-track  -   calls=1
FNOL-4473  deficiency  missing_date_of_loss   calls=0
FNOL-4478  deficiency  injury_reported   calls=1
FNOL-4479  deficiency  peril_not_covered   calls=1
FNOL-4481  deficiency  above_fast_track_limit   calls=1
```

**`FNOL-4473` costs nothing.** No date of loss, so no reading required, so no call — and the saving
is visible as a number rather than asserted as a design.

Now move the classifier call above `settled_without_reading` — which is the obvious refactor,
because it makes `decide` read top to bottom without a branch in the middle — and every one of those
five costs a call. On this queue that is four wasted calls out of twelve, or a third of the bill,
for a change that makes the function tidier and does not alter a single verdict. Nothing goes red.
Somebody would find it on the invoice.

The smallest fix is the ordering as printed. The general rule: **the expensive question goes last,
and "expensive" includes anything that has a rate limit attached to it.**

## In production

**What a senior writes instead.** The same order, and the saving measured rather than assumed —
`model=True` on every decision log line is what makes that possible here, and the number that
matters is the proportion of notifications that reach the classifier at all. They would also make
the budget's ceiling configurable rather than a constant, because two is a guess until a week of
real traffic has been through.

**What degrades at scale.** Two thirds of this queue needs the classifier, which at several hundred
notifications a day is several hundred model calls before any retry. The rules are free and the
model is not, and every rule that can be moved *before* the call is money — but the ordering is
bounded by correctness: `peril_not_covered` cannot move earlier, because it needs the peril. The
honest ceiling on this optimisation is the four early rules, and this desk is already at it.

**The review comment.** *"This calls the classifier first so the function reads more cleanly. Put it
back after the field rules — a third of this queue is settled without it, and that is a third of the
model bill for a tidier diff."*

**The interview question.** *"You have a cheap deterministic check and an expensive probabilistic
one, and both can settle the same case. How do you order them, and what would make you order them
the other way?"*
